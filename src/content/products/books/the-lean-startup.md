Eric Ries's The Lean Startup reshaped how a generation of founders think about building companies — favoring rapid experimentation and validated learning over long, unvalidated planning cycles.

## Why People Are Talking About It

The book's core concepts — the minimum viable product, build-measure-learn, and validated learning — have become standard vocabulary in startup and product circles well beyond the book itself.

## What Makes It Interesting

It reframes company-building as a series of testable hypotheses rather than a fixed plan, which appeals to founders working with limited time, money, or certainty.

## Who It's For

Founders, product managers, and anyone building a new product or business who wants a structured approach to reducing risk early.

## Key Features

- Introduces the minimum viable product framework
- Focused on validated learning and iterative experimentation
- Widely referenced across startup and product management circles

## Our Take

Even years after publication, this remains one of the most commonly recommended starting points for people building something new.

## Who Should Consider It

Founders, entrepreneurs, and product-focused readers looking for a structured approach to early-stage building.

## Who May Want Something Else

Readers looking for tactical, step-by-step execution guides rather than a foundational framework may want a more operational business book.
